These results show that the (1+1) Evolutionary Algorithm (EA) and Randomized Local Search (RLS) are consistently the top-performing algorithms across the benchmark suite, with Random Search trailing significantly behind in nearly every case. Both EA and RLS achieve the optimum in F1, F2, and F3, demonstrating their strength on linear and unimodal problems. In F18, a more deceptive and noisy landscape, EA slightly outperforms RLS, suggesting its mutation strategy offers better robustness in complex terrains. For F23 and F24, both EA and RLS again outperform Random Search, with EA showing a slight edge in final fitness. In F25, EA achieves the best result, with RLS close behind, while Random Search fails to meaningfully improve.

Judging by these results, EA appears to be the most consistently effective algorithm, particularly on rugged or deceptive problems. RLS is highly competitive and often reaches its highest fitness faster than the EA, but often falls short of the EA’s final fitness on account of its single-bit mutation strategy. Random Search, while simple, lacks the exploitation mechanisms necessary to perform well on structured problems and consistently underperforms.

Overall, EA and RLS are both well-suited for optimization tasks where solution quality and convergence reliability are important. EA’s adaptive mutation gives it a slight edge in complex landscapes, while RLS remains a strong option. Random Search, though useful for exploratory baselines, is not competitive in this context. These results reinforce the importance of guided search strategies in evolutionary computation, especially when tackling high-dimensional or deceptive problems.
