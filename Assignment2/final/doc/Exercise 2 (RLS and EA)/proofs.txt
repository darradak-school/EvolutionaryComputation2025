Let X be the number of times the optimal bitstring (all ones) is sampled during T = 2^n/2 evaluations of Random Search.

Each sample is independant and uniform over {0, 1}^N
probability of hitting the optimum in one trial is: 

p = 1 / 2^n

We want to bound the probability that at least one hit occurs
Using chernoff bounds, we get:

Pr[X = 0] ≤ e^(−μ) = e^(−2^(−n/2))

Therefore, the probability of success is:

Pr[X ≥ 1] = 1 − Pr[X = 0] ≥ 1 − e^(−2^(−n/2)).

Since 2^(−n/2) is exponentially small in n, we conclude:

Pr[success] ≤ 2^(−n/2) = e^(−Ω(n)),

hence

Pr[failure] ≥ 1 − e^(−Ω(n))

Random Search requires at least 2^(n/2) fitness evaluations to reach the optimum of the F1 function with probability at least 1 − e^(−Ω(n)). 
This shows that Random Search is exponentially unlikely to succeed within a sub-exponential budget.
